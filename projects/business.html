What this is

This section documents my understanding of how IT supports business processes. It comes from my Bachelor of Business Administration background combined with building practical IT systems that model real workflows.

What is ERP and why does it matter

ERP stands for Enterprise Resource Planning. It is software that helps companies manage their core processes in one place: orders, invoices, inventory, HR, reporting. Without ERP, companies rely on spreadsheets and manual processes that break under scale.

Companies like Prodware build ERP solutions on Microsoft Dynamics 365. The people who implement and support these systems need to understand both the business process and the technical system — that is exactly what I have been building toward.

How my projects model real business IT

AccessGate → Permission management

Every company has roles: admin, staff, guest, manager. Every role has permissions. AccessGate models exactly this logic. In a real ERP system, the same thinking decides who can approve invoices, who can edit records, and who can access reports.

ProcessFlow → Approval workflows

Business requests need approval based on rules. A small request gets auto-approved. A large one needs manager review. ProcessFlow models exactly this kind of business rule engine that ERP systems use daily.

IT Helpdesk → Service management

Every company needs a way to track IT problems: who reported it, what the issue is, how urgent it is, and whether it is resolved. This is called ITSM (IT Service Management) and my helpdesk system models the core of that workflow.

Incident Simulator → IT Operations

When something breaks in IT, there must be a structured response. Incidents are classified, prioritized, and escalated based on rules. My simulator models exactly how IT operations teams handle this.
